Ethereum Layer-2 Networks Keep Absorbing User Activity

Published: Aug 11, 2026  ·  Updated: Aug 13, 2026  ·  Author: Crypto News Editorial Desk

What happened

Industry dashboards and research outlets continue to report that a majority of everyday Ethereum-ecosystem transactions now settle on layer-2 rollups rather than on the mainnet itself. Lower fees and faster confirmation times have made rollups the default venue for consumer-facing applications, while the mainnet increasingly functions as a settlement and data-availability layer.

Why it matters

The migration of activity changes how the ecosystem should be evaluated. Metrics such as mainnet transaction counts understate real usage, while fee revenue is redistributed between layers. For developers, the choice of rollup now carries meaningful trade-offs around sequencer decentralization, exit mechanisms and tooling maturity.

Risks and caveats

Layer-2 networks inherit some — but not all — of Ethereum's security guarantees. Many still rely on centralized sequencers or upgrade keys held by small teams. Bridges between layers have historically been a frequent target of exploits. Users should understand the specific trust assumptions of any network they use.

Market context

Scaling progress is one of the variables analysts weigh when comparing smart-contract platforms. Competing ecosystems continue to pursue different architectures, and there is no consensus yet on which approach will dominate long-term.
